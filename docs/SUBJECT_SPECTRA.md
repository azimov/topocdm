# Per-subject spectra: MIMIC patients against topocdm synthetic patients

*A subject spectrum is the normalised Laplacian spectrum of one patient's condition–drug encounter graph. It is a per-patient object, so it can show a difference the cohort-level spectrum averages away. The comparison here is MIMIC patients against patients generated by topocdm fitted to MIMIC (the visit-anchored model).*

---

## 0. What the object is, and what it is not

Nodes are the **condition and drug concepts** a patient has. An edge means two concepts were recorded on the same day; edge weight is the number of days they co-occur. The normalised Laplacian is `L = I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}`; its sorted eigenvalues are the patient's fingerprint.

Why not all concepts? The all-concept graph is near-complete: one ICU visit can carry hundreds of measurements, so every concept is connected to every other, and the Laplacian spectrum collapses to a flat line at 1 with a spike at the ends. It looks identical for real and synthetic patients because it is the spectrum of a complete graph, not of the data. The condition–drug graph is sparse enough to have structure.

---

## 1. Examples

![Subject spectra examples](figs/fig31_subject_spectra_examples.png)

Top row: four MIMIC patients. Bottom row: four topocdm synthetic patients. Each line is one patient's sorted eigenvalues against rank fraction.

The visual difference is consistent across the examples. MIMIC patients have a **gradual rise from 0 to 1 over the first 10–20% of ranks**, then a flat region, then a **rise at the top end toward 1.3–2.0**. Synthetic patients jump almost immediately to 1 and stay there, with only a small excursion at the top.

A gradual low-eigenvalue rise means the graph has community or bipartite structure: conditions and drugs form groups that are internally connected and weakly connected to each other. The synthetic graphs are closer to complete: the model draws conditions and drugs independently within a visit, so every condition co-occurs with every drug and with every other condition in that visit. That is a complete bipartite/clique structure, and its spectrum is the flat line at 1.

## 2. Ensemble

![Subject spectra ensemble](figs/fig32_subject_spectra_ensemble.png)

| quantity | MIMIC | synthetic |
|---|---:|---:|
| patients with a graph | 100 | 73 |
| concepts per graph, median | 82 | 104 |
| algebraic connectivity `λ2`, median | **0.133** | **0.361** |
| largest eigenvalue `λn`, median | **1.333** | **1.100** |
| mean-spectrum L2 distance | — | 0.127 |
| pooled eigenvalue KS | — | 0.634 |

The synthetic graphs are **larger and more connected** (more concepts, higher `λ2`) and **less structured** (lower `λn`). The real graphs have the low-`λ2` / high-`λn` signature of a clustered, partly bipartite condition–drug network; the synthetic ones do not.

The mean spectrum is close (L2 0.127) because both are dominated by the flat region at 1. The difference lives in the tails and in `λ2`: exactly the part that carries the structure. This is the same failure as the cohort-level rank gap (37 against 61), now visible patient by patient.

---

## 3. What this means

The visit anchor fixed *when* events happen. It did not fix *what happens together*: conditions and drugs are drawn independently from their catalogues within a visit, so the synthetic condition–drug graph is denser and more homogeneous than the real one. The subject spectra show that directly, and they show it on the object the model is supposed to be good at.

This is a meaningful diagnostic, not a cosmetic one. It says the next layer must be a within-visit concept dependence — a topic or low-rank coupling on the visit catalogue — fitted on the condition–drug co-occurrence matrix. Until that is added, the model reproduces the encounter timing and the marginal concept frequencies, but not the subject-level network.

Caveat: 73 of 100 synthetic patients have a condition–drug graph with at least four nodes, against 100 MIMIC patients. The synthetic patients who are missing one are those whose generated visit contents were too sparse; that is itself part of the same under-structuring.
