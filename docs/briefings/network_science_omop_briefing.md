# Network science × OMOP epidemiology: reconnaissance briefing (2018–2026)

## 1. Multimorbidity / disease co-occurrence networks

**Methods.** Four families dominate. (i) *Phenotype co-occurrence with relative risk*: the template is [Hidalgo et al. 2009](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000353), who linked ICD-9-CM codes co-occurring within patients across 13M hospitalised Medicare enrolees and thresholded on RR — the "phenotypic disease network". (ii) *Adjusted odds-ratio networks*: [Aguado et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32051506/) built MorbiNet from 3.1M Catalan records using ORs adjusted for age and sex (edge if OR>1.2, p<1e-5). (iii) *Trajectory and community detection*: [Jensen et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24959948/) condensed 6.2M Danish registry patients into directed diagnosis pairs, and a large claims literature follows (e.g. [Zhou et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35052244/) on Chinese administrative data). (iv) *Graphical/Bayesian models* attempt partial-correlation or DAG recovery rather than pairwise co-occurrence; the ML framing is reviewed by [Hassaine et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32768443/).

**Criticisms.** The edge set is a *choice*, not a measurement. [Monchka et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35676621/) built seven networks from the same Manitoba administrative data (lift, RR, phi, Jaccard, cosine, Kulczynski, joint prevalence) and found the choice "significantly affected the number and structure of identified disease clusters", with different hub sets and little edge overlap once graphs were sparsified. [Brunson et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32607491/) replicated networks across seven datasets and found centrality rankings "highly sensitive to ontology", and — decisively — that under multivariate modelling "comorbid associations among low-prevalence disorders weakened while those between high-prevalence disorders shifted negative". That is the co-occurrence≠interaction problem made explicit: much of a disease network is shared healthcare contact. [Griffith et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33184277/) show the collider mechanism generally: conditioning on healthcare contact induces associations among its causes. Ascertainment is condition-specific even within one health system ([Kuan et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31650125/), 308 conditions in 4M NHS records). Age/sex are usually "adjusted" by stratification but rarely by design.

**Replication.** [Prados-Torres et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24472295/) reviewed multimorbidity pattern studies and reported heterogeneous solutions. I found **no** formal multi-database replication of a disease network with pre-registered edges — treat cross-database stability as unestablished.

## 2. Multiplex / multilayer formulations in epidemiology

**Actually done.** (a) *Age as layer*: [Dervić et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10920888/) is the strongest real example — 8 ten-year age layers over 8.9M Austrians, intra-layer co-occurrence plus inter-layer temporal links, overlapping communities as trajectories (1,260 trajectories; 70 diverging pairs). (b) *Evidence type as layer*: [Halu et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31044086/) multiplex the disease network over distinct association sources. (c) *Transmission route as layer*: the substantive programme is veterinary/zoonotic — [Kinsley et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33088828/) lay out multilayer host–pathogen analysis (direct vs indirect contact as layers) and apply it to US swine-farm contact data, showing that multilayer definitions change who looks like a superspreader. (d) Theory: multilayer spreading is well covered in [Pastor-Satorras et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.87.925).

**Proposed only.** Layers as *data sources* — primary care vs hospital vs prescription for the same patients — has, on my search, **not** been implemented for human multimorbidity; it is the clearest hole in this literature. Multiplex STI contact networks likewise remain largely theoretical: real STI network modelling is single-layer partnership data ([Eames & Keeling 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12271127/)).

## 3. Epidemic modelling on networks, and interference

Heterogeneity: [Pastor-Satorras & Vespignani 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11290142/) showed the epidemic threshold vanishes in infinite scale-free networks. **Assortativity claim**: [Boguñá et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12570587/) showed degree correlations control the threshold — assortative mixing restores a finite threshold in scale-free networks while disassortative mixing suppresses it; [Newman 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12241447/) gives the generating-function/percolation treatment and [Newman 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12636767/) the mixing-matrix formalism. Positive degree assortativity raises prevalence and epidemic size at fixed mean degree; clustering and concurrency do similar work in STI models ([Eames & Keeling 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12271127/); review: [Keeling & Eames 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16849187/)). Contact-matrix models fitted to real data: POLYMOD ([Mossong et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18366252/)) and its 152-country projection ([Prem et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28898249/)).

Interference: [Hudgens & Halloran 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19081744/) defined causal estimands under partial interference; [Aronow & Samii 2017](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-applied-statistics/volume-11/issue-4/Estimating-average-causal-effects-under-general-interference-with-application-to/10.1214/16-AOAS1005.full) give IPW estimation under arbitrary *known* exposure mappings; [Forastiere et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2020.1768100) extend identification to observational network studies. Note the ordering of difficulty: the statistics assumes you know the network.

## 4. What OMOP CDM gives you

Structure: `PERSON` (age/sex/race, `location_id`, `care_site_id`, `provider_id`), `OBSERVATION_PERIOD` (the denominator — critical and often abused), `VISIT_OCCURRENCE` (`visit_concept_id`, care site), `CONDITION_OCCURRENCE` (`condition_concept_id`, dates, `condition_status_concept_id`, visit link), `DRUG_EXPOSURE` (`days_supply`, `quantity`), `PROCEDURE_OCCURRENCE`, `MEASUREMENT` (`value_as_number`), `DEATH`, `NOTE`/`NOTE_NLP`; current version v5.5 ([OMOP CDM spec](https://ohdsi.github.io/CommonDataModel/); original validation: [Overhage et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22037893/); tutorial: [Book of OHDSI ch. 4](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/)).

For *networks* the key fact is negative: OMOP has **no household, family or contact edges**. The only relational structure is patient→provider, patient→care_site, patient→location, and patient→visit (a bipartite patient-event graph). Any patient–patient network must be built by projection (shared provider/site, shared geography) or by phenotype co-occurrence — which is exactly the ascertainment problem of §1.

Tooling: ATLAS, Achilles, DataQualityDashboard (>3,500 checks, mandatory before network studies), CohortDiagnostics, PatientLevelPrediction, PheValuator, CohortMethod; network studies run aggregate-only across >100 databases and >1e9 records ([Book ch. 20](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/NetworkResearch.html); [Hripcsak et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27274072/)). Data quality: [Kahn et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27713905/) harmonised terminology; [Blacketer et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34313749/) on DQD; phenotype misclassification is quantified semi-automatically by [Swerdel et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31369862/). PheCode mapping ([Wu et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31553307/)) is the usual phenotype layer.

## 5. Gaps and opportunities

**Publishable, and genuinely wanted.** (1) *Null models for co-occurrence networks*: a degree-corrected SBM or ERGM whose sufficient statistics include age, sex and visit count. This directly answers Monchka and Brunson and would land in JAMIA or BMC Medical Research Methodology. (2) *Ascertainment as a network sampling process*: formalise the patient–phenotype bipartite graph where an edge is observed only if care is sought, then state which network functionals are identifiable. This is the most original angle available. (3) *Layers as data sources*: multiplex SBM comparison of primary-care/hospital/prescription layers within one OMOP instance — quantifying shared vs layer-specific multimorbidity structure. Nobody has done it; it is a clean methods paper. (4) *Synthetic OMOP generator* with realistic comorbidity and utilisation structure for benchmarking — clearly publishable, OHDSI would adopt it. (5) *Interference in vaccine pharmacoepidemiology*: attractive but identification-poor; only credible with a defensible contact proxy (household via subscriber ID, shared care site). High risk.

**Would be dismissed as naive.** Community detection on co-occurrence edges, relabelled as "discovered phenotypes", with no utilisation adjustment and no external validation. Causal language over co-occurrence edges. Multiplex topology metrics (entanglement, modularity) reported with no epidemiological estimand, null model, or uncertainty. Generative models validated against the degree distribution alone rather than against held-out patients or a second database. Latent-space or hyperbolic embeddings presented as disease aetiology.

The rate-limiter in this field is identification and ascertainment, not model capacity. A generative-network skill set is most valuable spent on the observation process, not on the disease graph.
